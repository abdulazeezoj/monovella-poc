## Problem

### Produce rots before it reaches the market _(Agriculture)_

Smallholder farmers lose 30-40% of their harvest because there is no affordable cold storage between farm and market. Perishables spoil in transit, and one bad trip wipes out weeks of work.

**Example:** A tomato farmer in Kano loads a full truck for Lagos; heat and traffic spoil a third of it before arrival, so the trip barely breaks even.

Questions to consider:
- Which crops spoil fastest before they sell?
- Where is the loss highest - farm, transit, or market?
- What would a farmer pay to save one spoiled truckload?

### Farmers sell blind to middlemen _(Agriculture)_

Farmers rarely know the day's real market price, so middlemen buy low and resell high. Without price information they cannot plan planting or negotiate a fair deal.

**Example:** A maize farmer accepts ₦150/kg from a middleman, not knowing the Abuja market paid ₦230/kg that same morning.

Questions to consider:
- How does a farmer find today's price now?
- Who captures the margin the farmer loses?
- Would a farmer trust a price shown on a phone?

### Farmers can't tell real inputs from fake _(Agriculture)_

Counterfeit fertilizer and low-quality seeds flood rural markets, so farmers spend scarce cash on inputs that fail. A bad batch can cost an entire season.

**Example:** A rice farmer in Kebbi buys 'certified' seed from a roadside dealer; half never germinates and the season's yield collapses.

Questions to consider:
- Where do farmers buy inputs today?
- How would they verify an input is genuine?
- What does one fake batch cost over a season?

### Farmers can't prove their harvest to get credit _(Agriculture)_

Smallholders keep no records of what they grow or earn, so lenders and off-takers won't advance inputs or loans. Good farmers stay stuck at subsistence.

**Example:** A cassava farmer in Benue is refused input financing because she has no proof of last year's 8-tonne harvest.

Questions to consider:
- What record of harvest exists today?
- Who would lend if yield were verifiable?
- What's the smallest proof a lender would accept?

### Small businesses can't borrow without collateral _(Fintech)_

Informal traders and micro-SMEs have real cash flow but no land title or formal records, so banks reject them. The credit gap pushes them to expensive daily-interest lenders.

**Example:** A provisions-store owner turning over ₦400,000/month is refused a ₦100,000 bank loan for lack of collateral, and borrows from a street lender instead.

Questions to consider:
- What proof of income does this business already have?
- Where do they borrow when the bank says no?
- What data could stand in for collateral?

### Payments fail at the point of sale _(Fintech)_

On weak networks, card and transfer payments drop mid-transaction, so merchants lose the sale or can't tell if they were paid. Customers walk away and merchants stop trusting the tool.

**Example:** A boutique's POS shows 'transaction failed' but the customer's account is debited - the owner spends an hour confirming before releasing the goods.

Questions to consider:
- How often does a payment fail in a day?
- What does the merchant do when unsure they were paid?
- Who loses more when it fails - merchant or customer?

### Cross-border payments are slow and expensive _(Fintech)_

Freelancers and importers lose a big cut to fees and poor rates when money crosses borders, and transfers can take days. Getting paid from abroad is painful.

**Example:** A Lagos designer paid $500 by a foreign client receives far less after intermediary fees and a weak naira rate, days later.

Questions to consider:
- Who are they sending to or receiving from?
- What fee and delay do they accept today?
- What would fast and fair be worth to them?

### Informal workers have no safe way to save _(Fintech)_

Daily-income earners keep cash at home or with risky 'ajo' collectors - no interest and real theft risk. They stay invisible to formal finance.

**Example:** A danfo driver hands daily savings to an ajo collector who disappears with two weeks of contributions.

Questions to consider:
- Where do they keep money now?
- What do they fear most - theft, loss, or temptation?
- What would make them trust a digital wallet?

### Last-mile delivery is costly and unreliable _(Logistics)_

Small merchants can't offer dependable delivery - riders are ad-hoc, prices vary, and no one can track a package. Late or failed drops cost them repeat customers.

**Example:** A Lagos online seller loses a ₦20,000 order when the dispatch rider goes silent for two days and the customer cancels.

Questions to consider:
- How does the merchant arrange delivery today?
- What happens when a package goes missing?
- Would customers pay more for tracking they can trust?

### No one can track a package in transit _(Logistics)_

Once goods leave the shop, neither seller nor buyer can see where they are. Silence breeds disputes, chargebacks, and lost trust.

**Example:** An Aba manufacturer ships to Abuja but can't tell an anxious customer where the goods are, so the buyer refuses to pay on delivery.

Questions to consider:
- What does the buyer see after ordering?
- When do disputes start?
- What update would calm a waiting customer?

### Trucks return empty after one-way trips _(Logistics)_

Haulage is priced high because trucks often travel back empty - there's no easy way to match return loads. The cost passes to small shippers.

**Example:** A truck carries yams from Jos to Lagos, then drives back empty because the driver can't find a return load in time.

Questions to consider:
- How are loads and trucks matched today?
- Who pays for the empty return leg?
- What would fill a return trip reliably?

### Social-media sellers drown in DM orders _(Retail)_

Sellers run shops through WhatsApp and Instagram DMs, tracking orders, payments, and stock from memory. Orders get missed, stock counts go wrong, and payment confirmation is manual.

**Example:** A fashion vendor takes 30 orders across DMs during a sale, double-sells two items she no longer has, and has to refund angry customers.

Questions to consider:
- Across how many apps do orders arrive?
- How is stock counted between sales?
- What does one missed or double-sold order cost?

### Buyers can't trust secondhand goods online _(Retail)_

Used electronics and 'tokunbo' goods are sold sight-unseen on marketplaces, so buyers fear fakes and dead units. Fear kills conversion for honest sellers.

**Example:** A buyer in Ibadan pays for a 'clean' tokunbo laptop online and receives one with a swollen battery, with no recourse.

Questions to consider:
- How does a buyer judge quality before paying?
- What protects the buyer if it's faulty?
- How could an honest seller prove condition?

### Small shops overstock some goods and run out of others _(Retail)_

With no record of what sells, kiosks tie up cash in slow items and run out of fast-movers. Guesswork erodes already thin margins.

**Example:** A provisions shop in Enugu has cartons of unsold spaghetti but keeps running out of the sachet milk that sells daily.

Questions to consider:
- How do they decide what to restock?
- Which items sell out vs sit?
- What signal would tell them when to reorder?

### Shops keep records by hand and lose them _(SME)_

Micro-retailers track sales, credit, and expenses in paper notebooks that get lost, torn, or miscounted. They can't see what they're owed or whether today made a profit.

**Example:** A kiosk owner can't remember who took goods 'on book' last week because the page is smudged, so she never collects the debt.

Questions to consider:
- What do they record today, and where?
- Which number do they most wish they knew daily?
- What happens when the notebook is lost?

### Owners mix business and personal money _(SME)_

One pocket funds the shop and the home, so owners never know the true profit and quietly eat their capital. Growth stalls without clear numbers.

**Example:** A tailor in Kaduna uses the day's takings for school fees, then can't buy fabric for the next order.

Questions to consider:
- How is business cash separated from personal today?
- When do they realize the capital is gone?
- What would help them pay themselves cleanly?

### SMEs wait months to get paid by bigger clients _(SME)_

Small suppliers deliver to larger firms on credit and chase payment for months, starving their own cash flow. Late payment, not lack of sales, kills them.

**Example:** A cleaning-supplies SME in Port Harcourt finishes a ₦1.2M corporate order but waits 90 days for payment while wages fall due.

Questions to consider:
- Who are the slow-paying clients?
- What happens to the SME while it waits?
- What would make a client pay on time?

### Patients wait hours and can't reach care online _(Health)_

Clinics are crowded and far, and most telehealth assumes fast data and English. Patients on slow networks who speak Hausa, Yoruba, or Igbo can't easily get advice before traveling.

**Example:** A mother travels two hours to a clinic for a question a quick local-language chat could have answered.

Questions to consider:
- When would a patient prefer advice before traveling?
- What data speed and language do they actually have?
- Who do they ask now when the clinic is far?

### Small pharmacies run out of key drugs _(Health)_

Community pharmacies track stock by hand, so they over-order some drugs and run out of others. Expiry and stockouts mean lost sales and patients sent away.

**Example:** A pharmacy turns away three patients needing the same antimalarial in one day, unaware a batch expired unsold last month.

Questions to consider:
- How does the pharmacy know when to reorder?
- Which items expire unsold most often?
- What does a stockout cost in patients and trust?

### Patients can't tell real medicine from fake _(Health)_

Counterfeit and expired drugs circulate widely, so patients risk their health with every purchase from an unknown seller. Trust in medicine is low.

**Example:** A patient in Onitsha buys antimalarials from a busy open-market stall and gets no better - the pills were fake.

Questions to consider:
- Where do patients buy medicine now?
- How could they verify a drug is genuine?
- Who is hurt most by a fake dose?

### Community health workers track patients on paper _(Health)_

Frontline workers record pregnancies and immunizations in paper registers that can't flag who missed a visit. Follow-up slips, and risky cases go unseen.

**Example:** A health worker in rural Sokoto can't quickly tell which expectant mothers missed antenatal visits this month.

Questions to consider:
- What does the worker record today?
- Who falls through the cracks?
- What reminder would change an outcome?

### Students can't afford learning materials _(Education)_

Textbooks and exam-prep materials are expensive and physical, so students share or go without. Cost - not ability - blocks learning, especially outside the big cities.

**Example:** Three secondary-school students share one WAEC prep textbook, rotating it nightly before the exam.

Questions to consider:
- Which materials are hardest to afford?
- How do students cope without them today?
- What monthly price would feel fair to a student?

### Parents can't compare school quality _(Education)_

Parents choose schools on rumor and signboards, with no reliable data on results or fees. A bad choice costs years and money.

**Example:** A parent in Lagos pays a full term's fees before discovering the 'top' school has weak WAEC results.

Questions to consider:
- What does a parent rely on to choose now?
- Which facts would they trust?
- When is the decision made - and with what information?

### Graduates can't prove skills to employers _(Education)_

Certificates don't show real ability, so employers distrust them and capable youth get filtered out. Talent and jobs fail to meet.

**Example:** A self-taught developer in Abuja is skipped for interviews because his CV has no 'recognized' credential, despite shipping real apps.

Questions to consider:
- What does an employer actually want to see?
- How does a skilled person show proof today?
- What credential would both sides trust?

### Power cuts shut businesses down daily _(Energy)_

Grid power ('NEPA') is unreliable, so shops and small factories run costly generators or simply stop working when it's off. Diesel and fuel eat the profit.

**Example:** A barber in Ibadan loses half his customers on 'no light' days because his clippers and fan won't run without fuel for the generator.

Questions to consider:
- What stops working when the power goes?
- What do they spend on backup power?
- What uptime would change the business?

### Solar is out of reach for low-income homes _(Energy)_

Solar cuts long-term cost, but the upfront price blocks most households and there's little trust in installers. Families stay on candles and kerosene.

**Example:** A family in rural Kaduna wants solar but can't pay ₦250,000 upfront, so they keep buying kerosene every week.

Questions to consider:
- What do they pay for lighting now, weekly?
- Would pay-as-you-go fit their income?
- Who do they trust to install and service it?

### Rural internet is too slow to work with _(Energy)_

Outside the cities, data is slow and drops often, so heavy apps and video are unusable. Digital services must run light or lose these users entirely.

**Example:** A student in a Benue village can't load a video lesson that a Lagos student streams easily.

Questions to consider:
- What can and can't load on their network?
- When is connectivity worst?
- What would a low-data version need to do?

### Restaurants waste food they can't track _(Food)_

Kitchens over-buy perishables and bin what spoils, with no record of usage. Waste quietly erases the day's profit.

**Example:** A 'mama put' in Lagos throws out unsold jollof and stew nightly, never sure how much to cook the next day.

Questions to consider:
- What gets thrown away most?
- How do they decide how much to cook?
- What would right-sizing tomorrow's pot save?

### Food arrives cold or late from delivery _(Food)_

Restaurants rely on ad-hoc riders, so food arrives late or cold and customers blame the restaurant. Bad delivery loses repeat orders.

**Example:** An Abuja kitchen's ₦6,000 order arrives 90 minutes late and cold; the customer never orders again and leaves a bad review.

Questions to consider:
- Who delivers the food today?
- What goes wrong most - time, temperature, or wrong order?
- Who does the customer blame?

### Food vendors overpay for daily ingredients _(Food)_

Small vendors buy ingredients retail in tiny lots at high prices, with no group buying power. Thin margins get thinner.

**Example:** A suya spot in Kano buys pepper and onions daily at market retail, paying far more than a bulk buyer would.

Questions to consider:
- What do they buy daily, and where?
- How much cheaper is bulk?
- What blocks them from buying together?

### Renters lose money to fake agents _(Housing)_

House-hunting runs through informal agents who charge fees for listings that don't exist or aren't available. Renters pay to be scammed.

**Example:** A tenant in Lagos pays a ₦20,000 'inspection fee' to an agent for a flat that was already rented.

Questions to consider:
- How does a renter find a place now?
- Where does the money leak?
- What proof would make a listing trustworthy?

### Tenants can't afford a full year's rent upfront _(Housing)_

Landlords demand one to two years' rent at once, which most earners can't save. The lump-sum barrier forces bad housing choices.

**Example:** A young worker in Abuja earning monthly can't raise ₦1.2M for a year's rent, so she stays in an unsafe room far from work.

Questions to consider:
- How do tenants raise the lump sum today?
- Would monthly payment change their choice?
- What would make a landlord accept installments?

### Women entrepreneurs lack safe ways to sell _(Women and Family)_

Many women run businesses from home and face harassment or safety risks meeting buyers, limiting where and to whom they can sell.

**Example:** A home-based skincare maker in Kano avoids meeting unknown buyers alone, so she turns down orders that need pickup or delivery.

Questions to consider:
- Where and to whom can she sell safely?
- What risk makes her turn business away?
- What would let her sell without meeting strangers?

### Working mothers have no trusted childcare _(Women and Family)_

Affordable, trusted childcare is scarce, so mothers drop out of work or leave children in unsafe arrangements. Income and safety trade off.

**Example:** A market trader in Onitsha brings her toddler to a crowded stall daily because vetted daycare is unaffordable.

Questions to consider:
- Who watches the children now?
- What does she give up to work?
- What would trusted and affordable look like?

### Young people can't get a first job without experience _(Youth)_

Entry roles demand experience youth can't get, and there's no bridge from school to work. Capable graduates stall at home.

**Example:** An NYSC graduate in Enugu applies to 40 jobs; every one wants '2 years experience' she was never allowed to earn.

Questions to consider:
- What experience are employers demanding?
- How could a beginner build proof fast?
- Who would give the first chance, and why?

### Skilled youth can't find steady gig work _(Youth)_

Freelance and gig work is scattered across WhatsApp and word of mouth, so young talent has feast-or-famine income and no reputation to build on.

**Example:** A graphic designer in Jos gets one job this month from a friend, then nothing, with no platform to be found on.

Questions to consider:

- How does a newcomer get discovered?
- What would smooth the feast-or-famine?

### Youth can't access affordable tech training _(Youth)_

In-demand tech skills are taught in expensive city bootcamps, out of reach for most youth - especially outside Lagos and Abuja. Talent goes untrained.

**Example:** A secondary-school leaver in Maiduguri wants to learn coding, but the nearest bootcamp costs ₦500,000 in Lagos.

Questions to consider:

- What blocks access - cost, location, or device?

### Creators can't reliably get paid for their work _(Creative)_

Nollywood, music, and content creators struggle to earn from their audience - payments are informal, piracy is rampant, and platforms pay little or late.

**Example:** A skit-maker in Lagos with 200,000 followers earns almost nothing because brands pay slowly and his videos are re-uploaded for free.

- How does the creator earn today?
- Where does the money leak - piracy, fees, or delay?

### Plastic waste piles up with no easy way to recycle _(Climate)_

Households and markets generate plastic waste that clogs drains and streets, but there's no convenient collection or incentive to recycle.

### Families cook on smoky, costly fuels _(Climate)_

- What health toll do they notice?

## Revenue

### Direct Sales

**Formula:** Units sold x Selling price

**Typical use:** Goods, food, hardware, offline products

### Transaction Fee

**Formula:** Transactions x Avg transaction value x Fee rate

**Typical use:** Payments, fintech, marketplaces

### Commission

**Formula:** GMV x Commission rate

**Typical use:** Agents, travel, brokerage

### Subscription

**Formula:** Subscribers x Monthly price

**Typical use:** SaaS, education, media

### Retainer

**Formula:** Clients x Monthly retainer

**Typical use:** Services, B2B support

### Membership

**Formula:** Members x Membership fee

**Typical use:** Communities, clubs, associations

### Licensing

**Formula:** Licensees x License fee

**Typical use:** IP, software, content

### Pay-per-use

**Formula:** Usage units x Price per use

**Typical use:** APIs, mobility, utilities

### Metered Billing

**Formula:** Usage volume x Rate

**Typical use:** Energy, data, cloud

### Freemium

**Formula:** Paid users x Paid price - Serving cost

**Typical use:** Apps, SaaS, creator tools

### Tiered Pricing

**Formula:** Users per tier x Tier price

**Typical use:** B2B SaaS, education

### Marketplace Take Rate

**Formula:** GMV x Take rate

**Typical use:** Two-sided platforms

### Listing Fee

**Formula:** Listings x Listing fee

**Typical use:** Property, jobs, classifieds

### Lead Generation Fee

**Formula:** Leads x Fee per lead

**Typical use:** B2B marketplace

### Revenue Share

**Formula:** Partner revenue x Share rate

**Typical use:** Distribution partnerships

### Success Fee

**Formula:** Successful outcomes x Fee

**Typical use:** Recruiting, fundraising, claims

### Interest / Lending

**Formula:** Loan book x Annual interest / 12

**Typical use:** Lending, credit

### Float Revenue

**Formula:** Deposits held x Annual return / 12

**Typical use:** Wallets, savings

### Insurance Premium

**Formula:** Policies x Premium - Claims - Costs

**Typical use:** Insurtech

### Rental / Leasing

**Formula:** Assets rented x Rental fee

**Typical use:** Equipment, vehicles

### Asset Sale

**Formula:** Assets sold x Sale price

**Typical use:** Hardware, inventory

### Resale / Arbitrage

**Formula:** Units x (Sell - Buy - Logistics)

**Typical use:** Trading, commerce

### Factoring

**Formula:** Invoice value x Factoring fee

**Typical use:** SME finance

### Pay-per-outcome

**Formula:** Outcomes x Fee - Delivery cost

**Typical use:** Education, health, services

### Gain-sharing

**Formula:** Client savings x Share rate

**Typical use:** B2B efficiency

### Brokerage Fee

**Formula:** Deals closed x Fee per deal

**Typical use:** Real estate, trade

### Franchise Model

**Formula:** Initial fees + Monthly royalties

**Typical use:** Retail, food, education

## Metrics

### CAC _(Acquisition)_

Customer Acquisition Cost = acquisition spend / new customers

**Example:** Naira 100,000 marketing spend / 50 new customers = Naira 2,000 CAC

### Activation Rate _(Activation)_

Users completing first key action / signups x 100

**Example:** 120 completed onboarding / 300 signups = 40%

### Retention Rate _(Retention)_

Returning active users / previous active users x 100

**Example:** 80 returned out of 100 active users = 80%

### Churn Rate _(Retention)_

Lost customers / customers at start x 100

**Example:** 10 lost out of 200 = 5%

### MRR _(Revenue)_

Monthly Recurring Revenue = active subscribers x monthly price

**Example:** 300 subscribers x Naira 2,000 = Naira 600,000 MRR

### ARR _(Revenue)_

Annual Recurring Revenue = MRR x 12

**Example:** Naira 600,000 MRR x 12 = Naira 7.2M ARR

### GMV _(Marketplace)_

Gross Merchandise Value = total transaction value facilitated

**Example:** All sellers sold Naira 10M through the platform this month

### Take Rate _(Marketplace)_

Revenue / GMV x 100

**Example:** Naira 500,000 revenue / Naira 10M GMV = 5%

### TPV _(Fintech)_

Total Payment Volume = total value processed

**Example:** The payment app processed Naira 25M this month

### LTV _(Unit Economics)_

Average revenue per customer x gross margin x retention period

**Example:** Naira 2,000 x 60% x 12 months = Naira 14,400

### LTV:CAC _(Unit Economics)_

LTV / CAC

**Example:** Naira 14,400 LTV / Naira 2,000 CAC = 7.2x

### Gross Margin _(Finance)_

(Revenue - direct cost) / revenue x 100

**Example:** (Naira 1M - Naira 400K) / Naira 1M = 60%

### Runway _(Finance)_

Cash balance / monthly net burn

**Example:** Naira 5M / Naira 700K = 7.1 months

### Referral Rate _(Referral)_

Referred customers / total new customers x 100

**Example:** 30 referred users / 100 new users = 30%

### K-factor _(Referral)_

Invites per user x invite conversion rate

**Example:** 3 invites x 20% conversion = 0.6

### AOV _(Commerce)_

Average Order Value = revenue / orders

**Example:** Naira 500,000 / 250 orders = Naira 2,000

### Repeat Purchase Rate _(Commerce)_

Customers with 2+ purchases / total customers x 100

**Example:** 80 repeat buyers / 300 buyers = 26.7%

### Default Rate _(Lending)_

Unpaid balance / loan book x 100

**Example:** Naira 1M unpaid / Naira 20M loan book = 5%

### Course Completion _(EdTech)_

Completed learners / enrolled learners x 100

**Example:** 70 completions / 100 enrollments = 70%

### On-time Delivery _(Logistics)_

On-time deliveries / total deliveries x 100

**Example:** 920 on-time / 1,000 deliveries = 92%

## Tax

### VAT - Standard VAT Rate _(VAT)_

7.5% standard VAT rate under FIRS / Finance Act 2019.

**Example:** Invoice Naira 100,000 -> VAT Naira 7,500 if taxable.

### VAT - Exempt / Zero-rated _(VAT)_

Some basic food, medical, and educational materials may be exempt or zero-rated.

**Example:** Check whether the product category is VATable before pricing.

### Company Income Tax - Large Company _(CIT)_

30% on assessable profit for large companies above Naira 100M profit/turnover threshold in the reference sheet.

**Example:** Use for mature or high-revenue companies, not early micro-startups.

### Company Income Tax - Medium Company _(CIT)_

20% for medium companies with turnover around Naira 25M to Naira 100M.

**Example:** A growing SME should model this once revenue becomes meaningful.

### Company Income Tax - Small Company _(CIT)_

0% for small companies under the threshold in the reference sheet.

**Example:** Early-stage businesses may be exempt but should confirm eligibility.

### Education Tax _(Education Tax)_

3% of assessable profit according to Finance Act 2023 reference.

**Example:** Add to tax assumptions once the company becomes profitable.

### WHT - Dividends _(WHT)_

10% withheld at source on dividends paid.

**Example:** Relevant when distributing profit to shareholders.

### WHT - Interest Income _(WHT)_

10% on interest received from banks or deposits.

**Example:** Useful for float revenue or treasury assumptions.

### WHT - Rent Paid _(WHT)_

10% withheld on rent payments.

**Example:** Include when modeling office, shop, or warehouse rent.
