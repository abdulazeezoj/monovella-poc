# IP and Brand Registration

Two separate questions worth keeping distinct: does the company *own* its IP (covered in `references/formation-and-equity.md` — assignment from founders, contractors, and equity-based hires), and separately, has the company *registered* the protections that require registration to actually be enforceable. This file covers the second question.

## What's automatic vs. what needs filing

**Copyright** — automatic under Nigerian law (the Copyright Act) the moment an original work (code, written content, designs, illustrations) is created and fixed in a tangible form. No registration is required for the right to exist, though the Nigerian Copyright Commission offers a voluntary notification service that can help establish an evidentiary record of authorship and date, useful if authorship is ever disputed.

**Trademarks** — NOT automatic. Using a brand name or logo in commerce, even for years, does not by itself give exclusive legal rights to stop someone else from using something similar — a registered trademark does. Without registration, enforcement options against a copycat are meaningfully weaker and more expensive. This is directly relevant after any rebrand: the new name and mark are exactly the moment to file, before establishing years of unregistered use.

**Patents and industrial designs** — require registration (patents for genuinely novel inventions/processes; industrial designs for the visual appearance of a product) and involve a more technical, examiner-driven process than trademarks. Relevant if the company develops something more novel than "an app that does X" — a genuinely new technical process, device, or method.

**Trade secrets** — protected through confidentiality obligations (contracts) and actually keeping the information secret, not through registration. This is why confidentiality clauses in employment and contractor agreements matter as IP protection, not just as a formality.

## Trademark registration process (Nigeria) — verify current specifics before relying on them

The general process is consistent across sources: a preliminary clearance search (to check the name/mark isn't already registered or confusingly similar to an existing one), filing under the correct class using the Nice Classification system (45 classes — goods are 1–34, services are 35–45; a product with a mobile app, a wellness-adjacent service, and consulting work would likely need to consider more than one class), examination by the registering authority, publication in the official Trademarks Journal for a public opposition period, and — if unopposed or after opposition is resolved — certificate issuance. Registration is typically valid for seven years and renewable.

**A specific caution, not just a general one:** current online sources disagree noticeably on the name of the registering authority (variously described as the Trademarks Registry under the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment, "FIPO," or "NIPO"), and cite timelines ranging roughly 12-24 months and costs ranging from roughly ₦15,000 to ₦450,000+ depending on scope and whether an agent is used. This spread is wide enough, and the sourcing inconsistent enough, that none of these specific figures should be treated as reliable without verifying directly against the current registering authority's own published guidance (or a trademark agent's current quote) at the time of filing — this is a concrete example of the broader rule to verify rather than assume for anything time-sensitive.

## Practical priority for a startup

Register the trademark for the primary product/company name and logo once the brand is reasonably settled — registering before a major rebrand launch is cheaper and cleaner than registering after establishing an unregistered brand and then discovering a conflict. Copyright doesn't need active registration work but benefits from clear internal records (commit history, design file dates, written IP assignment from contributors) that would support a claim if one were ever needed. Patents/designs are worth raising only when something genuinely novel and technical is being built — most software products don't have patent-eligible subject matter in the way founders sometimes assume, and pursuing one without a real case is an expensive detour.

## When this needs a licensed IP lawyer or registered agent, not just this skill

The actual trademark search and filing (errors in classification or the application form are a common, costly way to lose the filing fee and delay protection), any opposition received during the publication period, any patent/design application, and any actual infringement situation (sending a cease-and-desist, or receiving one) should go to an IP lawyer or registered trademark agent rather than being filed directly from a draft.
